# Southwest Airlines

Southwest Airlines Co. is a Dallas-headquartered U.S. airline founded in 1966 by Rollin King and Herbert (Herb) Kelleher, incorporated in 1967 as Air Southwest Company and renamed in 1971.[^s2] It began scheduled service on June 18, 1971, with three Boeing 737s linking Dallas, Houston and San Antonio, after legal battles with incumbent carriers delayed its launch.[^s3][^s2] It is a standard business-school case because it turned a simple, consistently enforced low-cost position into decades of growth, and because it has recently abandoned several of the choices that defined that position.

## History

The airline started as an intrastate commuter service operating from Love Field near downtown Dallas, using "love" as a promotional theme.[^s2] It spread across Texas in 1975 and, after federal airline deregulation in 1978, expanded cautiously into neighbouring states, then into California, the Midwest, the East and the Southeast during the 1990s.[^s2] Kelleher's successors Colleen Barrett (president from 2001) and Gary Kelly (from 2008) led it through the industry's post-2000 crises.[^s2] As of December 31, 2025, it flew 803 Boeing 737s, still a single aircraft type, to 117 destinations, with about 72,790 full-time-equivalent employees.[^s3]

## The low-cost activity system

Michael Porter used Southwest in "What Is Strategy?" (1996) as his central example of strategic positioning. He describes it as short-haul, low-cost, point-to-point service between midsize cities and secondary airports, aimed at travellers who might otherwise drive or take a bus as well as convenience-seeking business flyers.[^s1] Its activities were tailored to that position: 15-minute gate turnarounds, no meals, no assigned seats, no interline baggage transfer, no premium cabin, direct gate ticketing that bypassed travel agents, and a standardized 737 fleet that simplified maintenance.[^s1] Britannica likewise credits the single-type fleet and ticketless travel for lower costs and faster turnarounds.[^s2]

Porter's key claim is about fit: each choice made the others work, so fast turnarounds depended on skipping meals, seat assignments and bag transfers, and on uncongested airports.[^s1] Imitating a few pieces gains little. His cautionary contrast is Continental Lite, which copied some features while keeping its full-service network, cut commissions and frequent-flyer benefits network-wide, and lost hundreds of millions of dollars, a "straddling" penalty.[^s1]

## People and coordination

Brandeis researcher Jody Hoffer Gittell offers a complementary explanation centred on relationships. In a twelve-month, nine-site study of flight departures at Southwest, American, Continental and United, stronger relational coordination (shared goals, shared knowledge and mutual respect across functions) correlated with fewer complaints, late arrivals and lost bags and with less staff time per passenger.[^s5] She reports that about 88% of Southwest's workforce was unionized yet it had the fewest strikes and mediations, and that it avoided layoffs after September 11, 2001, when rival majors cut about 16% of staff, which she ties to its practice of holding financial reserves.[^s5]

## Evidence and critiques

The case supports Porter's argument that durable advantage comes from a distinctive configuration rather than operational effectiveness alone, and Gittell's evidence suggests the culture was part of what rivals could not copy.[^s1][^s5] Critics note the model's fragility as the airline grew. In December 2022 operational failures cancelled about 16,900 flights and stranded more than two million passengers; on December 18, 2023 the U.S. Department of Transportation imposed a record $140 million civil penalty, and Southwest put its total cost at over $750 million.[^s6]

The sharper test is strategic. In 2025 Southwest introduced bag fees for most fares (from May 28), a basic economy fare, red-eye flights and new interline partners, and it began assigned and extra-legroom seating on January 27, 2026.[^s3] For FY2025 it reported record operating revenue of $28.1 billion but net income of $441 million, and guided 2026 adjusted EPS of at least $4.00.[^s4] In Porter's terms, the airline is giving up trade-offs that once locked out imitators in exchange for revenue its rivals already collect, so its experience will show whether the activity system or the brand was the real moat.[^s1][^s3]

## Connections

Southwest operates in, and is a narrower part of, the airline industry, which the Airline Deregulation Act opened to its interstate expansion.[^s2] It was founded by Herb Kelleher and Rollin King.[^s2] It is the textbook example of strategic positioning and the contrast case for operational effectiveness in Porter's work, and of relational coordination in Gittell's research, linking it to organizational culture.[^s1][^s5] Its single-type fleet and high aircraft utilisation tie it to economies of scale, and Porter's fit argument explains how its activity system acted as a barrier to imitation.[^s1]
