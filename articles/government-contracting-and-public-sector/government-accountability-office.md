# Government Accountability Office (GAO): The Congressional Watchdog Over Federal Spending

The **Government Accountability Office (GAO)** is the audit, evaluation and investigative agency of the US Congress. It sits in the legislative branch, deliberately outside the executive departments it examines, and describes itself as independent and nonpartisan.[^s2][^s3] Its work centers on how the executive branch spends federal money and whether programs achieve their goals; it is headquartered in Washington, D.C., with 11 field offices.[^s3] For government contractors, GAO matters in two ways: its audits shape acquisition policy, and it is the main administrative forum for bid protests.[^s2]

## Origin

Congress created the agency as the **General Accounting Office** through the Budget and Accounting Act of 1921, after World War I had swollen federal spending and debt.[^s1] Until the end of World War II its job was largely checking the legality of expenditures and auditing vouchers.[^s1] As postwar programs grew it shifted toward helping Congress monitor executive programs; in 1974 Congress widened its evaluation and budget roles, prompting it to hire scientists, actuaries and policy experts, and in 1986 it formed a team of investigators.[^s1] In 2004 it was renamed the Government Accountability Office (P.L. 108-271) to reflect that performance audits, not bookkeeping, had become most of its work.[^s1][^s2]

## How it works

GAO's authorities, as summarized by the Congressional Research Service (CRS), include auditing and evaluating federal programs, investigating misuse of public funds such as contract fraud, providing legal services to Congress, resolving bid protests, and setting auditing standards and executive-branch accounting principles.[^s2] It has broad statutory access to agency records and can sue a noncomplying agency, though that power is rarely used; a federal court in *Walker v. Cheney* (2002) held it lacked standing to compel records from the Vice President's energy task force.[^s2]

Its recommendations are not binding. Agency heads must, however, send a written statement of the action taken or planned, and Congress enforces follow-through with hearings, appropriations leverage and legislation.[^s5] As of October 2026 CRS counted more than 5,000 open GAO recommendations, about 450 of them designated priority.[^s5] GAO also polices the power of the purse: under the Impoundment Control Act it reviews presidential withholdings of funds, reports unreported impoundments and may sue to force release.[^s7] Between July 2025 and October 2026 its listed decisions found violations, in whole or part, in six of ten cases, including NIH grants (August 2025) and FEMA assistance (September 2025).[^s7]

GAO is led by the **Comptroller General**, nominated by the president from at least three names recommended by a bipartisan, bicameral congressional commission, confirmed by the Senate, and serving a single 15-year term; removal requires impeachment or a joint resolution for specified causes.[^s2]

## Evidence and critiques

GAO reported $62.7 billion in financial benefits for fiscal 2025, alongside 671 products, 1,833 new recommendations and 46 congressional testimonies; its High Risk List then held 38 areas.[^s4] Britannica notes its long reputation for objectivity, with criticism usually aimed at particular findings rather than the agency's integrity.[^s3]

Scholars are more cautious about how it is used. Selin and Moore, studying 6,248 GAO documents sent to the two main oversight committees from 1999 to 2021, found that committees relied on GAO markedly less over time and argued that requests are rarely neutral, since legislators tend to commission reviews that fit their views.[^s6] CRS has flagged structural weaknesses: lengthy Comptroller General vacancies, including 1996-1998, staff cuts of about 39% between 1992 and 1998, and disputes over access to intelligence agencies.[^s2]

## In practice

The appointment process itself became contested in 2026. Gene Dodaro finished his 15-year term at the end of 2025, and Orice Williams Brown has served as acting Comptroller General since.[^s8][^s4] In November 2026 Senate Republicans moved to hold a hearing on a nominee without using the statutory recommending commission, drawing objections from Democrats and from former Comptroller General David Walker.[^s8]

## Connections

GAO is an instrument of **congressional oversight** within **public administration**, created by the **Budget and Accounting Act of 1921** and headed by the **Comptroller General**.[^s1][^s2] It supplies committees with information about executive agencies that they would otherwise have to gather from other sources;[^s6] in principal-agent terms, that narrows the **information asymmetry** between Congress and the agencies acting on its behalf, linking it to the **principal-agent problem**. It is the main venue for **bid protests** in **public procurement**, and its audits inform the choice of **government contract types**.[^s2] It contrasts with agency **offices of inspector general**, which sit inside the executive branch and report every six months on unimplemented recommendations.[^s5] By testing whether programs deliver results, it gives Congress evidence relevant to **public value**.[^s3]
