# Statement of Cash Flows: Sources and Uses of Cash by Activity

The statement of cash flows is one of the primary financial statements. It reports the cash a business received and paid out during a period and explains why its cash and cash equivalents changed between the opening and closing balance sheets.[^s2] Where the income statement answers whether a company earned a profit under accrual accounting, the cash flow statement answers whether that activity actually produced cash, and the two figures can diverge sharply.[^s3] Investors, lenders and managers use it to judge liquidity, the ability to fund investment internally, and the quality of reported earnings.[^s1]

## Origin

In the United States the statement became mandatory when the Financial Accounting Standards Board issued Statement of Financial Accounting Standards No. 95 in November 1987, after roughly six years of work; the guidance now sits in Accounting Standards Codification Topic 230.[^s4] SFAS 95 replaced the statement of changes in financial position required by APB Opinion 19, which often tracked "funds" defined as working capital and was criticised because companies defined funds inconsistently.[^s4] Internationally the equivalent rule is IAS 7, maintained by the International Accounting Standards Board.[^s2]

## How it works

Both GAAP and IFRS sort cash flows into three activities.[^s2]

- **Operating activities** are the cash effects of the transactions that produce net income: collections from customers and payments for inventory, wages, utilities and taxes.[^s1]
- **Investing activities** cover buying and selling long-term assets such as property, plant and equipment and investments that are not cash equivalents; under IAS 7 this includes obtaining or losing control of subsidiaries.[^s1][^s2]
- **Financing activities** change the size and mix of equity and borrowings: issuing or repaying debt, issuing stock, repurchasing shares and paying dividends.[^s1][^s2]

Operating cash flow can be shown two ways. The direct method lists gross cash receipts and payments by major class; the indirect method starts from profit and adjusts for non-cash items such as depreciation, for changes in working capital accruals and deferrals, and for items that belong in investing or financing.[^s2][^s3] FASB has always encouraged the direct method, but preparers overwhelmingly use the indirect method, citing the cost of capturing gross cash data.[^s4] Significant non-cash investing and financing transactions, such as buying a building with a note payable, are excluded from the three sections and disclosed separately.[^s1][^s2] The standards also require companies to define cash equivalents (short-term, highly liquid investments with insignificant risk of value change) and reconcile them to the balance sheet.[^s2]

The standards continue to evolve. The IASB added disclosures on changes in financing liabilities (2016) and supplier finance arrangements (2023), and IFRS 18 (2024) makes the new operating-profit subtotal the starting point for the indirect method and changes how interest and dividends are classified.[^s2] In the US, FASB issued ASU 2016-15 on eight specific classification issues and ASU 2016-18 on restricted cash.[^s4]

## Evidence and critiques

The statement's analytical value is clearest in research on earnings quality. Richard Sloan's 1996 study of 40,679 NYSE and AMEX firm-years from 1962 to 1991 found that the accrual component of earnings is less persistent than the cash flow component, yet stock prices behaved as if investors did not distinguish the two.[^s5] A portfolio long low-accrual and short high-accrual firms earned size-adjusted returns of about 10.4% in the first year, an effect later known as the accrual anomaly.[^s5]

Critics point to weaknesses in the format itself. Classification of interest and dividends, the boundaries of cash equivalents and restricted cash, and gross versus net presentation remain contested, and regulators and inspection reviews have repeatedly found classification errors in practice.[^s4] The dominance of the indirect method also means users rarely see gross operating receipts and payments, which the FASB considers more informative.[^s4]

## In practice

Apple's statement for the three months ended December 27, 2025 illustrates the reconciliation. Net income of $42.1 billion was adjusted upward for non-cash charges including $3.2 billion of depreciation and amortization and $3.6 billion of share-based compensation, giving cash generated by operating activities of $53.9 billion.[^s6] Investing activities used $4.9 billion, including $2.4 billion for property, plant and equipment, while financing activities used $39.7 billion, chiefly $24.7 billion of share repurchases and $3.9 billion of dividends.[^s6] The pattern (operating cash well above profit, modest capital spending and large returns to shareholders) is exactly what analysts read from the three sections.[^s6]

## Connections

The statement is a core input to financial statement analysis and the source of operating cash flow and capital expenditure, the components of free cash flow.[^s1] It is governed by GAAP (ASC 230) and IFRS (IAS 7), and it complements the income statement by translating accrual accounting results back into cash, with working capital changes and non-cash charges such as those excluded from EBITDA forming the bridge.[^s2][^s3] It succeeded the statement of changes in financial position, and its operating cash data underlie Sloan's accrual anomaly research.[^s4][^s5] Apple's quarterly filing exemplifies its use.[^s6]
