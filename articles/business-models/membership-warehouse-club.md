# Membership Warehouse Club

A membership warehouse club is a retail format in which customers pay an annual fee for the right to shop, and in return buy from a deliberately narrow assortment of food and general merchandise, frequently in bulk packs, at thin markups in large, plain, self-service buildings.[^s1] The model splits the retailer's income into two streams: a fixed membership fee and a small margin on each sale. The operator can therefore price goods closer to cost than a supermarket or discount store while still earning a profit.[^s2]

## Origin

The format is credited to Sol Price, a San Diego lawyer who had earlier run Fed-Mart, a discount store open only to government employees. With his son Robert he opened the first Price Club on July 12, 1976, in a converted aircraft hangar in San Diego.[^s1] Price Club first sold to small businesses, limiting membership to holders of business licences or resale permits, charged an initial annual fee of $25, and later widened eligibility to groups such as government and credit-union employees and finally to ordinary consumers.[^s1] Price preferred the lowest markup to the deepest discount, and he avoided sales, gimmicks and even the word "discount".[^s1] Imitators followed, including Costco, Walmart's Sam's Club and BJ's; Price Club merged with Costco in 1993.[^s1]

## How it works

Five design choices reinforce one another. First, a limited assortment: Costco says it carries fewer than 4,000 active items per warehouse in its core business, whereas an average grocery store stocks roughly 50,000 items.[^s2][^s4] Fewer items mean larger orders per supplier, faster inventory turnover and simpler logistics. Second, low-cost operations: no-frills, self-service warehouses averaging about 148,000 square feet.[^s2] Third, low markups, which let the club run at gross margins well below most retailers.[^s2] Fourth, the membership fee itself, which in fiscal 2026 brought Costco $5.9 billion, against net sales of $297 billion and net income of $9.2 billion.[^s2] Costco's 10-K calls the membership format integral to both its business and its profitability.[^s2] Fifth, loyalty mechanics: Costco reported 84.1 million paid members at the end of fiscal 2026 and renewal rates of 92.3% in the U.S. and Canada (89.8% worldwide); its higher-fee Executive tier, which earns a reward on qualifying purchases, accounted for about half of paid members and roughly three-quarters of net sales.[^s2]

Economists describe this pricing as a two-part tariff: a fixed access fee plus a per-unit price. The more a member buys, the lower the effective cost of the fee per purchase, which pushes members to concentrate their spending at the club.[^s6]

## Evidence and critiques

Research on warehouse clubs points two ways. Courtemanche and Carden found that a new Costco is associated with higher, not lower, prices at nearby grocery stores (about 1.4% in the short run and 2.7% in the long run), while Sam's Club entry had no detectable effect. They suggest that incumbents may respond by targeting less price-sensitive shoppers or by competing on amenities.[^s4] On the consumer side, Ailawadi, Ma and Grewal used household scanner panels around club openings and found that club shopping substantially raised the quantity of packaged food bought per person. The extra volume was concentrated in storable and impulse items, so it added calories, sugar and saturated fat without changing nutritional quality.[^s5] Bulk formats can therefore increase consumption rather than simply lowering the price of a fixed basket, which complicates the claim that members always save. The model also concentrates profit in fee renewals, so a fall in renewals hurts more than a dip in item margins.[^s2]

## In practice

Costco is the clearest example. At the end of fiscal 2026 it ran 939 warehouses (647 in the U.S. and Puerto Rico, 115 in Canada and 177 elsewhere) and attributed about 30% of that year's growth in membership income to the fee increase that took effect in the U.S. and Canada on September 1, 2024.[^s2] Sam's Club U.S. reported net sales of $93.0 billion for Walmart's fiscal year ended January 31, 2026, and $2.5 billion in membership and other income, up 8.7%.[^s3]

## Connections

The club is a specific business model built on pricing strategy: its two-part tariff sets it apart from the razor-and-blades model, which subsidises an entry product and profits on refills, whereas the club profits on the access fee and holds item margins down.[^s6] Its recurring fee links it to the subscription business model and makes customer lifetime value, driven by renewal rates, the key economic metric.[^s2] Volume purchasing and a narrow assortment are an application of economies of scale, and studies of its effects on rival grocers belong to industrial organization.[^s4] Sol Price is the originator; Costco and Sam's Club are the main exemplars.[^s1]
