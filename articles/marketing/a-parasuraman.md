# A. Parasuraman

**A. "Parsu" Parasuraman** is a marketing scholar best known for turning service quality from a loosely defined idea into something firms could model and measure. With Valarie A. Zeithaml and Leonard L. Berry he developed the gaps model of service quality (1985) and the SERVQUAL survey instrument (1988), and he later built widely used scales for customers' readiness to adopt technology and for the quality of service delivered through websites.[^s1] He has described the service quality work with Zeithaml and Berry as the contribution he is proudest of.[^s2]

## Career

Parasuraman trained first as an engineer, earning a B.Tech. in mechanical engineering at IIT Madras in 1970, then an MBA in marketing from IIM Ahmedabad in 1972 and a doctorate in business administration from Indiana University in 1975.[^s1] After teaching at the University of Northern Iowa, he spent 1979–1994 at Texas A&M University, rising to Federated Professor of Marketing, and in 1994 moved to the University of Miami as holder of the James W. McLamore Chair in Marketing; he became emeritus in 2019.[^s1] At Miami he also chaired the marketing department and served as vice dean of faculty.[^s1] He edited the *Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science* (1997–2000) and the *Journal of Service Research* (2005–2009).[^s1] In an American Marketing Association interview he traced his interest in marketing to his engineering background and to the interface between companies and customers, and dated his collaboration with Berry and Zeithaml to the early 1980s.[^s2]

## Contributions

**Service quality.** The 1985 *Journal of Marketing* article with Zeithaml and Berry proposed a conceptual model of service quality, and the 1988 *Journal of Retailing* article introduced SERVQUAL, a multiple-item scale for customers' perceptions of service quality; the team refined the scale in 1991 and re-examined expectations as a comparison standard in 1994.[^s1] Their book *Delivering Quality Service* (1990) carried the research to managers, and Parasuraman also wrote *Marketing Services: Competing Through Quality* (1991) with Berry.[^s1]

**Technology readiness.** In 2000 Parasuraman published the Technology Readiness Index (TRI) in the *Journal of Service Research*, and with Charles Colby wrote *Techno-Ready Marketing* (2001).[^s1] The construct is a person's propensity to embrace and use new technologies at home and at work, built from two motivators (optimism and innovativeness) and two inhibitors (discomfort and insecurity).[^s4] In 2014 Parasuraman and Colby cut the original 36 items to a 16-item TRI 2.0, tested on 878 U.S. adults, and used it to segment consumers into five groups ranging from Skeptics (38%) to Pioneers (16%).[^s4]

**Online service quality.** E-S-QUAL, published in 2005 with Zeithaml and Arvind Malhotra, measures how well a website enables efficient and effective shopping, purchasing and delivery.[^s3] Its 22 items cover efficiency, fulfillment, system availability and privacy, while a companion 11-item E-RecS-QUAL scale covers responsiveness, compensation and contact for customers who had a problem or needed help.[^s3]

## Evidence and critiques

Parasuraman's measurement work drew influential challenges. Cronin and Taylor argued in 1992 that the gap-based operationalization of service quality confused satisfaction with attitude, and found that a performance-only measure could do better.[^s5] Buttle's 1996 review noted that SERVQUAL had become a widely adopted tool for measuring and managing service quality since its launch, but catalogued theoretical and operational concerns about it and proposed a research agenda.[^s6] Parasuraman's own later scales also report limits: the TRI 2.0 authors caution that the two inhibitor dimensions have weaker convergent validity,[^s4] and E-S-QUAL's authors call E-RecS-QUAL preliminary because small samples restricted its validation.[^s3]

## Recognition

His honours include the American Marketing Association's Career Contributions to the Services Discipline Award (1998), Paul D. Converse Award (2012) and Gilbert A. Churchill Award (2013), election as an AMA Fellow (2016), the Academy of Marketing Science's Outstanding Marketing Educator award (2001), and an honorary doctorate from Maastricht University (2011).[^s1] *The Quality Review* listed him among the ten most influential figures in quality for 1988, and Clarivate named him a Highly Cited Researcher in 2014, 2017, 2019 and 2020.[^s1]

## Connections

Parasuraman sits within **services marketing**, the field his work did much to define.[^s2] He **influenced** the **gaps model of service quality** and its measurement instrument **SERVQUAL**, whose node records him as creator, and both tie **service quality** to **customer satisfaction**, the link Cronin and Taylor contested.[^s1][^s5] He also **influenced** the **Technology Readiness Index** and **E-S-QUAL**, which extend the same measurement approach to technology adoption and to service delivered online.[^s4][^s3]
